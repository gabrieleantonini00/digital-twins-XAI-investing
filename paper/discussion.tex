The primary three-way interaction was not statistically detectable ($\chi^2(1)=0.90$, $p=0.344$), providing no clear support for joint moderation in this run. Proportional-odds diagnostics flagged departures involving literacy, limiting interpretation of the common odds ratios.

The planned contrasts describe where the fitted model places differences between the two disclosure descriptions. They do not identify emotional mediation, observed trust, or investment performance. Nor does a significant comparison in one market and a nonsignificant comparison in the other demonstrate that the two effects differ.

Baseline separation makes the inferred literacy characterization independent of exposure to the assigned vignette. However, the outcome request explicitly includes that characterization, so any moderation can partly reflect the model responding to its own prior labels. A human study with independently measured financial knowledge is needed to evaluate whether the simulated heterogeneity corresponds to investor behavior.

The observations represent one sampled response from each distinct persona under one fixed model configuration. The experiment was not designed to estimate within-person response variability or compare temperatures. The inferential uncertainty reported here is conditional on the chosen profiles, prompts, model, and configuration.
